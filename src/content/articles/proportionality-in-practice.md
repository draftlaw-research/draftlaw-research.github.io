Proportionality review is often described as a single test, but in practice it is a sequence of questions. Each question narrows the space in which a court can disagree with the legislature.

## The usual structure

Most formulations ask four things in order: whether the measure pursues a legitimate aim, whether it is suitable to achieve that aim, whether a less restrictive measure would do equally well, and whether the benefits outweigh the harm to the right ([example source](https://example.org/source)).

The first three steps look like fact-finding. The fourth is where values enter openly, and it is where reasoned disagreement between judges is most visible.

> A balancing exercise can be transparent without being predictable.

![](/src/assets/covers/balance.jpg)

## Why the last step matters

Courts differ on how much weight to give the legislature's judgment at the final stage. A reader who only looks at outcomes will miss that the disagreement is usually about *deference*, not about the existence of the right.
