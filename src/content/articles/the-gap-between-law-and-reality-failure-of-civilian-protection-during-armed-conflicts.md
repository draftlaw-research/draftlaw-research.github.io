<span style="color: black">War is often described in terms of military victories, territorial control and political interests. However, war causes destruction on an unimaginable scale. Behind every armed conflict are civilians who lost their homes, livelihoods, families and, worst of all, their lives. Ordinary people often bear the most serious consequences of war.</span>

<span style="color: black">This is particularly concerning because international law provides rules to protect civilians during the armed conflict. Yet</span> [<span style="color: black">gravest human rights violations are committed  across the world</span>](https://www.amnesty.org/en/what-we-do/armed-conflict/)<span style="color: black">.</span>

> <span style="color: black">This raises an important question, If international humanitarian law (IHL) provides rules for protecting civilians, why do they continue to suffer most during an armed conflict?</span>

[<span style="color: black">According to the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC)</span>](https://www.icrc.org/sites/default/files/document/file_list/what-is-ihl-factsheet.pdf)<span style="color: black">, international humanitarian law is a set of rules which seek, for humanitarian reasons, to limit the effects of armed conflict. It protects persons who are not or are no longer participating in the hostilities and restricts the means and methods of warfare. International humanitarian law is also known as the law of war or the law of armed conflict. The four Geneva Conventions of 1949 form the foundation of this legal framework. They establish protections for wounded, sick and shipwrecked members of the armed forces, prisoners of war and civilians affected by armed conflict. Yet the continuing suffering of civilians demonstrates the gap between the promises of international law and their implementation.</span>

## <span style="color: black">**The International Legal Framework for the Protection of Civilians**</span>

<span style="color: black">International humanitarian law is based on several fundamental principles designed to protect civilians during armed conflict.</span>

<span style="color: black">**The first is the Principle of Distinction**, under</span> [<span style="color: black">Article 48 of Additional Protocol I to the Geneva Conventions (1977)</span>](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/api-1977/article-48?activeTab=) <span style="color: black">parties to the conflict must distinguish between civilians and combatants and civilian objects like homes, schools, hospitals apart from military targets.</span> [<span style="color: black">Article 51</span>](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/api-1977/article-51?activeTab=) <span style="color: black">bans direct attacks on civilians and civilian objects.</span>

<span style="color: black">**The second is the Principle of Proportionality,** under</span> [<span style="color: black">Article 51(5)(b)</span>](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/api-1977/article-51?activeTab=)  <span style="color: black">even when an attack is directed at a legitimate military objective, it is prohibited if the expected civilian deaths, injuries or damage to civilian objects would be excessive compared with the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated.</span>

<span style="color: black">**The third is the Principle of Precaution,** which adds practical duties under</span> [<span style="color: black">Article 57</span>](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/api-1977/article-57?activeTab=)<span style="color: black">, parties to the armed conflict must do everything feasible to confirm that the target is military, choose means and methods that reduce civilian harm and cancel or suspend an attack if it becomes clear that it would violate international humanitarian law.</span>

<span style="color: black">Together, these rules demonstrate that military actions must have limits, even during war.</span> [<span style="color: black">The ICRC explains the rules governing the conduct of hostilities and the role of IHL in the protection of civilians.</span>](https://www.icrc.org/en/law-and-policy/conduct-hostilities-and-protection-civilians)

## <span style="color: black">**International Recognition of Civilian Protection**</span>

<span style="color: black">The United Nations Security Council has repeatedly recognized the importance of protecting civilians during armed conflict. One important example is</span> [<span style="color: black">UN Security Council Resolution 1674 (2006)⁠</span>](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1674\(2006\))<span style="color: black">, which reaffirms the responsibility of parties to armed conflict to comply with international humanitarian law and international human rights law. It also addresses the protection of civilians, humanitarian access and the responsibility to protect populations from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing and crimes against humanity.</span>

<span style="color: black">Similarly,</span> [<span style="color: black">Resolution 1894 (2009)</span>](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/atf/cf/%7B65BFCF9B-6D27-4E9C-8CD3-CF6E4FF96FF9%7D/POC%20SRES1894.pdf)<span style="color: black">⁠ demands compliance with international humanitarian and human rights and refugee law, condemns attacks on civilians, opposes impunity, urges accountability, reparation and strengthens peacekeeping protection mandates and seeks to safeguards humanitarian access and aid workers.</span>

<span style="color: black">The Security Council has also addressed specific threats to civilians. The</span> [<span style="color: black">Resolution 2286</span>](https://docs.un.org/en/s/res/2286\(2016\)) <span style="color: black">condemns attacks on medical facilities and personnel.</span> [<span style="color: black">Resolution 2575</span>](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2575\(2021\))  <span style="color: black">(2021) addresses attacks against critical civilian infrastructure, while</span> [<span style="color: black">Resolution 2730</span>](https://docs.un.org/en/S/Res/2730\(2024\)) <span style="color: black">(2024)⁠ Focuses on the protection of humanitarian personnel, United Nations personnel and associated premises and assets.</span>

<span style="color: black">These resolutions show that civilian protection is not a new concern in international relations. It has remained on the international agenda for decades. Yet, the repeated need for such resolutions also raises questions about why existing commitments have not translated into consistent protection on the ground.</span>

## <span style="color: black">**Why Does Civilian Protection Continue to Fail?**</span>

<span style="color: black">Despite these legal protections, civilians continue to face major harm during war. Several factors help explain this gap.</span>

<span style="color: black">**First, modern warfare increasingly takes place in populated areas.** Fighting in such areas exposes civilians to greater risks because military objectives may be near homes, schools, hospitals and other infrastructure. Attacks in such areas can cause widespread damage, even when the intended target is military.</span> [<span style="color: black">The UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine</span>](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167935) <span style="color: black">verified 1,396 civilians killed and 7,978 injured in the first half of 2026, a 37 per cent rise on the same period in 2025. Its head attributed the rise to powerful weapons that are especially deadly in densely populated urban areas.</span> [<span style="color: black">The presence of armed groups among civilian populations does not remove the obligation of opposing forces to distinguish between civilians and military</span>](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/customary-ihl/v2/rule7?country=ch#sectionb)<span style="color: black">.</span>

<span style="color: black">**Second, accountability remains difficult.** International law can establish responsibility, but enforcing that responsibility is far harder. Investigations may be delayed or prevented by the ongoing hostilities, a lack of reliable evidence, political pressure and unwillingness of the parties to cooperate. Powerful states may also face fewer practical consequences because of their political influence and strategic relationships.</span> [<span style="color: black">The International Criminal Court (ICC) can prosecute individuals for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and the crime of aggression within its jurisdiction</span>](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1261&context=ilsajournal)<span style="color: black">, but it does not have its own police force to arrest suspects.</span>

<span style="color: black">**Third, political interests often influence international responses.** The security council has primary responsibility for maintaining international peace and security, but the five permanent members hold “Veto” power. Political considerations can prevent strong and consistent action, even when serious violations are reported.</span>  [<span style="color: black">In September 2025</span>](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/09/1165881)<span style="color: black">, a draft resolution on Gaza received 14 votes in favor and one against. It was not adopted because the United States, a permanent member, voted against it. The draft would have demanded an immediate, unconditional and permanent ceasefire and that Israel lift restrictions on aid deliveries. Vetoes are not limited to one state. All five permanent members of the UN Security Council possess veto power and there is no limit on how many times a permanent member can use its veto.</span>

<span style="color: black">**Finally, the nature of warfare is changing.**</span> [<span style="color: black">The use of artificial intelligence, drones, cyber systems and advanced weapons creates new challenges for civilian protection</span>](https://www.civilianprotectiontrends.org/prevent)  <span style="color: black">during armed conflict.</span> [<span style="color: black">Technology may improve military accuracy, but it can also create risks when decisions are made using incomplete information or systems that are not sufficiently transparent</span>](https://www.civilianprotectiontrends.org/prevent)<span style="color: black">.  The use of these advanced weapons in warfare raises several complex legal, ethical and humanitarian questions, particularly concerning accountability, scale of harm and the enforcement of IHL. New technology does not remove existing legal obligations. Parties to armed conflict must continue to comply with the principles of distinction, proportionality and precautions.</span>

## <span style="color: black">**Is International Humanitarian Law Ineffective?**</span>

<span style="color: black">It would be simplistic to conclude that international humanitarian law has completely failed. Without these laws, civilians would have far fewer legal protections during armed conflict. The Geneva Conventions establish minimum standards for the protection of civilians, and provide a legal basis for holding individuals and states accountable.</span> 

<span style="color: black">The Security Council resolutions also demonstrate that civilian protection remains a recognized international legal responsibility. n standard against which the conduct of parties to armed conflict can be assessed. It gives humanitarian organizations, courts and states a legal framework to identify violations and demand accountability.</span>

<span style="color: black">However, there is a difference between having laws and ensuring that they are followed. International humanitarian law cannot independently stop a military operation, arrest a responsible commander or force a government to comply. Its effectiveness depends heavily on the willingness of states, international institutions and parties to armed conflict to respect and enforces their obligations.</span>

## <span style="color: black">**What Needs to be Improved?**</span>

<span style="color: black">Improving civilian protection requires more than adopting additional resolutions. Existing legal commitments must be translated into meaningful action.</span>

<span style="color: black">States must ensure that violations of international humanitarian law are investigated independently and effectively. Those responsible for war crimes must be held accountable, regardless of their political or military position.</span>

<span style="color: black">Furthermore, the international community must respond to violations consistently. Political positions, alliances and strategic interests should not determine whether civilian suffering receives attention. The Security Council should also work towards more effective and timely responses when civilians face serious threats. Humanitarian organizations must also be allowed to reach affected populations safely.</span>

## <span style="color: black">**Conclusion**</span>

<span style="color: black">International humanitarian law was developed with a clear intention- to ensure that even during war, humanity is not completely abandoned. It recognizes that civilians should not become targets simply because they live in a war zone. Yet the continuing destruction of civilian lives and infrastructure reveals a serious gap between legal commitments and actual practice. The Geneva Conventions, including Common Article 3, Security Council resolutions and decisions of the International Court of Justice (ICJ) provide a strong legal foundation for civilian protection. The challenge is ensuring that these protections are respected in practice and that violations lead to meaningful consequences.</span>
