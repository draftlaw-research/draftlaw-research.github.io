## **About the program**

Each cycle, experienced researchers bring an original research idea and work with 2 to 3 undergraduate co-authors over a period of 4-6 months. Together they take a project from conception to journal-ready submission, learning how to identify a research question, build an argument, write for publication, and navigate the academic process.

## **For mentors**

### **What We’re Looking For**

We welcome mentors from law and multidisciplinary backgrounds, including sociology, political science, economics, psychology, technology/AI, environmental science, medicine/public health, philosophy, business, journalism etc. as long as your proposed research connects meaningfully to law.

### **As a Mentor, Your Responsibilities Include:**

- Proposing a research topic you’d like to develop with students
- Guiding 1–2 assigned students from scratch; through topic refinement, literature review, methodology, and argument-building
- Actively teaching the research and academic writing process, not simply assigning tasks
- Reviewing student work diligently and responding within reasonable timeframes
- Co-authoring the final paper, with full and fair credit given to student contributions
- Seeing the project through to completion, and notifying DraftLaw Research in advance if genuine unforeseen circumstances require stepping back
- Maintaining confidentiality of research materials and drafts until formal publication
- Engaging professionally and respectfully with students and fellow mentors throughout

### **Important to Know**

- This is a **voluntary, unpaid** mentorship role; DraftLaw Research does not generate financial gain from this program, and no fees are charged to students or mentors.
- Final papers are submitted for publication in an international journal of the mentor’s choosing.
- Mentors may be based anywhere in the world, this is a fully remote collaboration.

### **Why Join Us?**

This is a chance to shape the next generation of legal thinkers, develop your own research ideas with dedicated student support, and contribute meaningfully to a growing academic community, all while building real mentorship experience.

### **How to apply**

Please keep an eye out for an open call for mentors in the announcements section of our website or social media handles. 

## **For students**

### **What we look for**

We welcome undergraduate law students with genuine curiosity for legal research, a willingness to learn, and the commitment to see a project through from start to finish. No prior research experience is required, our mentors will guide you from scratch.

### **Responsibilities**

- Working closely and consistently with your assigned mentor throughout the project
- Actively learning and applying the fundamentals of academic legal research 
- Completing assigned tasks diligently and within agreed deadlines
- Communicating promptly if you're unable to meet a deadline or complete a task
- Maintaining confidentiality of research materials and drafts until formal publication
- Engaging respectfully and professionally with your mentor and fellow collaborators
- Committing to the project through completion, and seeking permission in advance if genuine unforeseen circumstances require stepping back

### **Who can apply**

Undergraduate or fresh graduate or postgraduate law students from any jurisdiction or region can apply, since this is a remote opportunity.

### **Important to Know**

- This opportunity is **completely free** — no fees at any stage.
- You'll be credited as **co-author** on the final paper, alongside your mentor.
- The paper will be submitted for publication in a reputable international journal.
- Successful participation can be a strong addition to your CV and a real edge for LL.M. admissions and scholarship applications abroad

### **How to apply**

Please keep an eye out for an open call for mentors in the announcements section of our website or social media handles.
