# **How to Make the Best Rebuttal and Surrebuttal**

A rebuttal is a short speech in which you answer the other side’s case. It is often the part judges remember best, because it shows that you listen, think fast and understand the whole case.

- **Rebuttal:** usually given by the Applicant or Claimant after the Respondent has spoken.
- **Surrebuttal:** in some moots, a short reply from the Respondent to the rebuttal.

Rules differ on who gives which, how long it lasts, and whether you must ask for the time in advance. Check them.

## **The rules of a good rebuttal**

1. **Do not repeat your main speech.** Answer what the other side said, not what you already said.
2. **Choose, do not cover everything.** You have little time. Pick what matters most.
3. **Attack the argument, never the person.** Be polite and calm. Say “my learned friend” and “respectfully”.

### The formula: 

```
Name, Break, Bring back
```

- NAME       : "My learned friend submitted that ..."   (state it fairly and exactly)
- BREAK      : "Respectfully, that is wrong / incomplete because ..."   (source, fact or logic)
- BRING BACK : "So the Court should still find that ..."   (link to your case)

Each point takes about 20 to 30 seconds. In a two-minute rebuttal, make **three** points. In a one-minute rebuttal, make **two** or one strong point.

### How to choose what to answer

Pick from this list, in this order:

1. **A clear mistake** the other side made, especially one the judges may have missed.
2. **Their strongest point,** if you can answer it well. This shows courage.
3. **A new point** they raised that you have not answered yet.

Skip the middle points. Skip anything, you have already answered in your main speech. Skip tiny errors.

## **Types of rebuttal points**


|  |  |  |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Type** | **How to do it** | **Example** |
| Wrong fact | Give the paragraph number | “Counsel said Ulka warned Torsa. Paragraph 8 records no warning.” |
| Wrong law | State the correct rule with a source | “Counsel said sovereignty is absolute. The Court said otherwise in *Corfu Channel*.” |
| Misused source | Take the judge to another passage of the same case | “Counsel cited *Pulp Mills* at [101]. The same judgment at [204] requires an environmental assessment before the project, which Ulka never did.” |
| Case is different | Say why the difference matters legally | “*Case X* concerned a boundary river with a joint commission. There is none here.” |
| Gap in logic | Show the step that is missing | “Counsel says the email was valid. He did not say how a ministry official could receive notice for the State.” |
| Inconsistency | Show two points do not fit | “Counsel first said the treaty ended in 2023, then said Article 4 still applied in 2024.” |
| Use their concession | Repeat what they accepted | “Counsel accepted that flow fell below 40 percent. That ends the breach question.” |
| Unanswered question | Remind the judges | “The Court asked about the required form of notice. Counsel did not answer.” |


## **Taking notes while they speak**

Draw a line down the middle of a page.


|  |  |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Left: what they said | Right: my reply |
| Exact words, plus the point in their memorial (if known) | Short plan: law, fact or logic |


Mark the three best points with a star (★). Write their exact phrases, because repeating a phrase back is effective: “Counsel said, and I quote, ‘a State may do what it likes with its water’. Respectfully, no State has such a right.” (Only quote if you are sure of the words. If you are not, say “as the Applicant understood counsel’s submission”.)

## **Prepare before the round**

You cannot know exactly what the other side will say, but you can predict most of it.

1. Build a **rebuttal bank**: the 10 best arguments the other side could make, each with a 30-second reply.
2. Keep each reply on one card, with the source and pinpoint.
3. Practice saying them in Name, Break, Bring back form.
4. If you have the other side’s memorial before the round, mark its weakest and strongest points.

### Pre-emptive rebuttal (before they speak)

- If you know what the other side will argue, you may answer it inside your main submissions. Rules for this:
- First make your own positive argument. Then contrast it with theirs.
- Describe their point fairly. Do not attack a weak version that they did not make (a “straw man”).
- If you have not seen their written case, say “may argue”, never “will argue”.
- Do not let your whole speech become a response to them. You need to prove your own case.

## **Structure of a rebuttal**

One-line opening : "My learned friend made three points. I answer each briefly."

Point 1          : Name, Break, Bring back.

Point 2          : Name, Break, Bring back.

Point 3          : Name, Break, Bring back.

Closing          : One sentence of your theme + what you ask the Court to do.

### Example: a two-minute rebuttal (Torsa answering Ulka)

> *“My Lords, my learned friend made three points. First, he said Article 12 does not specify the form of notice, so an email is enough. Respectfully, Article 12 says written notice to ‘the other party’. The other party is the State of Torsa, not one junior official. His reading would let any State end a river treaty by an unseen message, which defeats the treaty’s purpose.*
>
> *Second, he said Ulka is free to use water within its own borders. Respectfully, that freedom stops where serious harm to another State begins. Corfu Channel and Pulp Mills say so. It is not disputed that salt water entered Torsa’s farms after the dam closed (paragraph 10).*
>
> *Third, he said the harm is not proved to come from the dam. Respectfully, the facts state that the salt water arrived in the dry season after the flow fell. My learned friend gave no other cause.*
>
> *For these reasons, the treaty remains in force, Ulka broke Article 4, and Torsa asks the Court to order restoration of the flow and compensation.”*

### Example: a short surrebuttal (Ulka)

A surrebuttal answers only what is new in the rebuttal.

> *“My Lords, two short points. First, counsel for the Applicant now says notice must go to the State. The treaty does not say so, and the Applicant gave no authority. Second, counsel says salt water followed the dam. The facts do not rule out natural causes in a dry year, and the Applicant has not proved otherwise. Ulka asks the Court to dismiss the claim.”*

Notice: short, calm, only two points.

## **Useful phrases**

- “My learned friend submitted that… Respectfully, that is not correct, because…”
- “With respect, counsel cited [Case] at paragraph [X]. The passage that matters is at paragraph [Y].”
- “Counsel did not answer the Court’s question about…”
- “Counsel accepted that… That is enough to decide this issue.”
- “It is respectfully submitted that this point does not assist the Respondent, for two reasons.”

## **Do not do these**

- Do not repeat the same argument you made before. It sounds like you have nothing new.
- Do not introduce a brand-new argument unless the rules allow.
- Do not exaggerate or call their case “absurd”. Show why it fails.
- Do not misquote the other side. If you are unsure, say “as we understood it”.
- Do not exceed your time.
- Do not panic if you cannot answer everything. Two clean points are better than six rushed ones.

## **Practice drill: the 60-second rebuttal**

Watch a recorded round, or have a teammate speak for three minutes on the other side. Take notes in two columns. Then give a 60-second rebuttal using Name, Break, Bring back. Time it. Ask a teammate: Did you answer the best point? Was it fair? Did you link it to the final result? Repeat ten times with different topics.
