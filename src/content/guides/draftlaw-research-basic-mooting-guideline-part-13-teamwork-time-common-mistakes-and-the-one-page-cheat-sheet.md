# **Teamwork, Time, Common Mistakes and the One-Page Cheat Sheet**

Talent helps, but teams usually win on habits: they start early, work in a clear system, practice a lot and support each other.

## **Build the team system in week one**

Sit together and agree on a short team charter:

- **Roles.** Who leads research on which issue? Who edits? Who manages deadlines, fees and travel?
- **Meetings.** Fix a regular meeting time, and a short check-in message on the other days.
- **One shared folder.** Same structure for everyone, for example: 1 Problem and clarifications; 2 Notes and timeline; 3 Sources; 4 Drafts; 5 Final files; 6 Oral prep.
- **File names.** Use dates and versions (Memorial_Torsa_v7_2026-12-03). Never keep ten copies with names like “final final”.
- **Decision rule.** How do you decide when two people disagree? For example: discuss for 15 minutes, check the best source, then the coach decides.
- **Feedback rule.** Criticize the work, not the person. Give feedback in writing and in person, kindly and clearly.

## **A season plan (from problem release to the end of the oral rounds)**

Lengths depend on the moot. Use the same logic.


|  |  |  |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stage** | **Share of time before the memorial deadline** | **Goal** |
| Read and note | First 10% | Three readings, shared note, clarification questions |
| Learn and research | Next 35% | Background reading, source log, theory of the case |
| Outline and draft | Next 35% | Headings, then paragraphs, issue by issue |
| Review and fix | Next 15% | Peer review, style pass, citations |
| Checklist and submit | Last 5% | Final checklist, upload early |
| Oral practice | Starts the day after submission | Follow the practice timeline |


## **Use your coach and your network well**

Ask your coach for **specific** feedback: “Is my heading for Issue 2 clear?” works better than “Is this fine?”

Set dates for the coach to read drafts.

Invite alumni, teachers, and former mooters to your mock rounds. Sometimes a short message and a clear request (“We need two judges for a 90-minute mock on Saturday”) is usually welcomed.

Join or form a moot society at your university. Keep your memorials, notes and feedback sheets so that the next team learns from you.

Ask for help early on funding, visas and travel. Chambers, alumni, university departments and companies sometimes sponsor teams. Prepare a one-page team profile and a clear budget.

## **Balance study, health and moot**

- Plan around your exams. Tell teachers early.
- Fix a weekly rest day.
- Sleep at least six to seven hours a week before a deadline. A tired mind makes silly mistakes in citations and facts.
- Eat properly before a round. Drink water.

## **The 15 most common beginner mistakes**


|  |  |
| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Mistake** | **Fix** |
| Reading the problem once | Three readings (Part 2) |
| Ignoring jurisdiction and admissibility | Always check them first |
| Starting to write before researching | Outline, then source log, then draft |
| Citing a source you never opened | Open and read everything you cite |
| Trusting AI-written citations | Check every source on an official site |
| Using too many weak sources | Three strong sources beat ten weak ones |
| No pinpoints | Always give a paragraph or page |
| Describing the law without applying it to the facts | End each paragraph with the application |
| Adding or changing facts | Use only the problem and clarifications |
| Long, old-style sentences | Short sentences, plain words |
| Calling the other side’s case “absurd” | Show why it fails |
| Reading a script in the oral round | Outline with bullets and key quotes |
| Speaking too fast | Slow down, pause, breathe |
| Not preparing both sides | Write an “enemy summary” |
| Leaving the memorial upload to the last hour | Upload at least six hours early |


## **The mindset of a best mooter**

- **Curious.** You enjoy hard questions and read beyond the minimum.
- **Honest.** You admit weak points and never invent sources.
- **Flexible.** You change your plan when the judge’s questions require it.
- **Calm.** You treat nerves as energy, not as danger.
- **Generous.** You help teammates and are polite to opponents.
- **Resilient.** You treat a bad round as a lesson. Every champion lost rounds when they started.

A moot is not only about winning. It teaches you how to think, research, write and speak under pressure. Those skills last your whole career.

## **The one-page cheat sheet**


|  |  |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stage** | **Remember this** |
| Read | Three readings: Story, Question, Detective |
| Note | **FACT + SO WHAT? + WHICH SIDE?** Seven boxes: parties, timeline, issues, gaps, contradictions, traps, questions |
| Research | Background, then primary sources, then scholarship. Source log with pinpoints. Weight: Binding? Relevant? Recent? Respected? |
| Theory | One or two sentences that tell your whole case |
| Argue | **CREAC**: Conclusion, Rule, Explanation, Application, Conclusion. Over, around or through the wall |
| Headings | [Subject] + [action] + [because legal reason] |
| Memorial | Pleadings first. Facts and summary last. Plain English, short sentences |
| Check | Last 72 hours plan. Anonymity. Metadata. Upload early. Download and check |
| Oral prep | Priority points 1 to 4. Notes not script. Question bank. Mock rounds |
| Open | Greeting, who you are, teammate, offer facts, **roadmap with claims** |
| Each point | Signpost, claim, why it matters, rule and source, application, mini-close |
| Questions | **Listen, pause, answer, back it up, bridge back** |
| Don’t know | “I regret I am unable to assist the Court on that point.” |
| Rebuttal | **Name, Break, Bring back.** Two or three points. Strongest or weakest first |
| Close | One line per submission. Prayer for relief. “Unless I can assist further…” |
